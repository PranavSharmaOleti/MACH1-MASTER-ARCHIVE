# MACH1 External Research Audit

**Research cut-off:** 4 November 2026  
**Geography:** India  
**Scope:** 41 external research gaps, prioritising demand validation, fleet buyers, airport operations and operating economics.

> **Evidence discipline:** A company having a fleet, an airport permitting its vehicles, and a company actually procuring vehicles are three different facts. The findings below distinguish published claims, official records, secondary-source estimates and primary-research gaps. “Verified” applies only to the specific fact stated, not to every related claim.

**Citation note:** Source reference IDs (for example, `turn293234view0`) are preserved from the research conversation. They are conversation citation references, not standalone URLs. The source titles are included alongside them so they can be located in the original conversation and used to retrieve the underlying links.

## 2. Market size and demand data — items 7–15

### 2.1 India ride-hailing market

A *Business Standard* article published on 7 May 2026 attributes to Redseer an estimate of approximately **3 billion ride-hailing trips in CY2025**, with booking value of approximately ₹55,000 crore.

The article reports the following trip mix:

| Mode | Share of trips |
|---|---:|
| Cabs | 27% |
| Two-wheelers | 28–30% |
| Three-wheelers / autos | Approximately 45% |

Source: **Business Standard — India's ride-hailing market, 7 May 2026** (`turn885994view3`; related `turn293234view5`).

What this establishes and does not establish:

- The reported 27% is a share of trips, not GMV.
- `3.0 billion × 27% = 810 million`. Thus, 810M cab trips is an arithmetic derivation from the 3B total and 27% share, not a separately verified Redseer disclosure.
- The cab figure should not automatically be interpreted as app-based four-wheeler taxi trips. The accessible article does not provide sufficiently detailed category definitions.
- The accessible source does not establish how completed trips, cancellations, pooled rides, rentals or intercity journeys are treated.

The original Redseer report and its methodology were not independently available in the retrieved sources. Retain **3B total / approximately 810M cab trips as a secondary-source estimate**, not Grade A market data.

### 2.2 Motilal Oswal daily ride figures

The January 2025 Motilal Oswal material is traceable through its published research article:

| Platform | Reported daily cab trips |
|---|---:|
| Uber | 840,000 |
| Ola | 460,000 |
| Rapido | 320,000 |
| **Total** | **1,620,000** |

Source: **Motilal Oswal — ride-hailing market research** (`turn629389view6`; related `turn294033search3`).

The figures are reported as daily cab trips, but the accessible publication does not establish whether they are representative daily averages, a particular month's run rate or peak observations. It also does not adequately define Rapido's vehicle-class scope.

Do not use these figures to calculate market share without resolving period and denominator. Rapido's overall business includes bike taxis, autos and cabs; total rides cannot be treated as four-wheeler cab rides.

### 2.3 Airport passenger traffic

The AAI public traffic portal's latest available monthly report at the 4 November 2026 cut-off was August 2026. An official September 2026 AAI report containing the claimed April–September figure was not verified in the retrieved sources (`turn126260search4`; `turn463741search0`).

A secondary compilation of monthly AAI passenger figures reports:

| Month | Domestic + international passengers |
|---|---:|
| April 2026 | 33.83M |
| May 2026 | 36.98M |
| June 2026 | 33.39M |
| July 2026 | 30.63M |
| August 2026 | 31.01M |
| September 2026 | 30.10M |
| **Sum of displayed monthly figures** | **195.94M** |

Source: **IndiaDispatch — monthly airport passenger traffic** (`turn701929view1`; related `turn242141search0`).

This is a sum of displayed, rounded secondary figures. It does **not** verify the exact 196.24M number. It is also not a count of unique passengers. A passenger arriving at one airport and later departing from another can contribute to multiple passenger movements. The movement count needs to be separated into terminating passengers, transfer passengers and transit passengers before serving as a ground-access demand denominator.

Recommended report wording:

> Approximately 196 million domestic and international passenger movements were reported across Indian airports during April–September 2026, based on secondary compilation of monthly AAI figures; the exact 196.24M figure and its detailed passenger-category composition have not been confirmed against a September AAI release.

### 2.4 Airport road-mode share

No defensible national app-cab share was verified. The assumed 20–30% should not be used as an observed national statistic.

One India-specific study is a 2025 airport ground-access mode-choice study at Guwahati Airport:

- Sample: 700 respondents.
- Survey design: random sampling over 15 consecutive days.
- Method: multinomial logit mode-choice analysis.
- Findings include differences in ridesourcing propensity by age and visitor status.

The published abstract reports, for example, that visitors were more likely than local residents to choose ridesourcing. The accessible abstract does not provide an actual airport-wide ridesourcing mode-share percentage. It cannot validate a national 20–30% assumption.

Source: **The evolving dynamics of airport ground access: mode choice at Guwahati Airport, 2025** (`turn547930search0`).

### 2.5 Status of items 11–15

| Item | Finding | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 11. Road-eligible passenger share | Not established nationally. Total passenger movements are not equivalent to terminating passengers requiring road transport. | Airport-level arrival/departure and transfer segmentation; mode-choice survey |
| 12. App-cab mode share | No nationally representative measured share found. | Airport operator counts, platform data or structured passenger survey |
| 13. App-cab occupancy | No reliable national airport occupancy figure found. | Passengers per booked vehicle for airport trips, separated by solo/group booking |
| 14. Offline taxi volume | No consolidated national count found that reliably isolates non-platform taxi journeys. | Taxi permits/registrations plus airport and city-level trip counts |

**Airport demand calculation warning:** Do not multiply AAI passenger movements by an assumed 20–30% app-cab share and call the result addressable app-cab trips. That combines an unverified passenger denominator, an unverified mode share and potentially double-counted passenger movements.

## 3. Segment operating data — items 16–21

**Evidence status: Grade D / unverified.** No public source retrieved independently validates the seven-number bundles in the positioning deck. They should remain hypotheses, not market facts.

| Item | Segment | Values in the deck | Research disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Intra-city | 12.7 km/trip; 95 km/day; 7.5 trips/day; ₹350/trip; 18% growth; 10% EV share; 0.85 price sensitivity | All seven unverified |
| 17 | Commute | 22 km; 176 km/day; 8 trips/day; ₹550; 24% growth; 12% EV; 0.90 sensitivity | All seven unverified |
| 18 | Airport | 35 km; 210 km/day; 6 trips/day; ₹600; 26% growth; 18% EV; 0.35 sensitivity | All seven unverified |
| 19 | Intercity | 280 km; 340 km/day; 1.5 trips/day; ₹4,200; 20% growth; 5% EV; 0.40 sensitivity | All seven unverified; 340 vs 420 km/day unresolved |
| 20 | Premium | 18 km; 144 km/day; 8 trips/day; ₹520; 28% growth; 15% EV; 0.30 sensitivity | All seven unverified |
| 21 | Intra-city share | 61% / approximately 58% | No verified numerator, denominator or classification method |

### 3.1 Intercity inconsistency

The values imply:

`280 km/trip × 1.5 trips/day = 420 km/day`

That is inconsistent with 340 km/day if both numbers refer to the same vehicle, operating day and trip definition.

Possible explanations include: 280 km is a one-way route while the vehicle completes a different number of revenue legs; trip count includes legs differently; or 340 km uses a different operating-day definition. None can be selected without the original data. Do not silently change 340 to 420, or vice versa.

### 3.2 Required definitions for a valid segment dataset

| Measure | Required definition |
|---|---|
| Trip distance | Passenger journey distance, not vehicle movement |
| Paid kilometres | Distance generating revenue |
| Deadhead kilometres | Empty repositioning to pickup, depot or next trip |
| Total vehicle kilometres | Odometer/GPS distance over the operating shift |
| Waiting time | Time available but not earning |
| Charging downtime | Plug-in, queueing and charging time, separately |
| Daily trips | Completed passenger journeys, not booking requests |
| Fare | Gross passenger fare, net of discounts and platform deductions |
| Occupancy | Passengers actually carried per vehicle journey |
| Growth | Defined segment, geography, baseline and forecast period |
| EV share | EVs as a share of a defined vehicle stock, new registrations or completed trips |
| Price sensitivity | Estimated response to price changes, not a qualitative opinion |

For airport trips, collect inbound and outbound journeys separately. Airport trips can have materially different deadhead, queueing, waiting and toll patterns. Fare-per-trip comparisons without these factors can misstate the opportunity.

**Recommendation:** Keep all 35 segment values out of the evidence-backed market-sizing calculation until the underlying source is supplied or a GPS/driver-day study is completed. Price-sensitivity coefficients must be estimated from actual price variation or a properly designed choice experiment; the five coefficients in the deck are not defensible as observed elasticities.

### 4.1 Evera and the BluSmart vehicle question

The “10% residual, realised 26%” statement has a traceable source. In a September 2024 interview, BluSmart co-founder Anmol Jaggi said Xpres-T cars were leased on a 10% residual assumption and were then fetching close to 26% in the resale market.

Source: **Mint — BluSmart co-founder on electric-vehicle resale values, 5 September 2024** (`turn629389view7`).

This is a published management statement, not an audited residual-value study. It does not disclose VINs, vehicle age, kilometres, condition, sale date, actual sale price, original acquisition cost or denominator.

Safe report wording:

> BluSmart management claimed close to 26% resale value versus a 10% lease residual assumption.

Do not state that BluSmart's fleet independently realised a 26% residual value.

The SEBI order records that Gensol had obtained loans for 6,400 EVs, and that purchase confirmations from Go-Auto covered 4,704 vehicles for ₹567.73 crore. The order raises serious concerns about the deployment and use of the borrowed funds. Treat the findings as regulatory findings/allegations in the relevant proceeding, not as a blanket final adjudication of every vehicle's disposition. (`turn418822view9`; `turn462963view0`.)

A later arrangement reported in December 2025 concerned a lease of **4,000 vehicles** from the insolvency process to Signodrive/Evera. This is not proof that all 4,704 cars were sold, or that all 4,000 were actively operating by November 2026. (`turn462963view3`; `turn462963view4`.)

**Unresolved:** No public VIN-level reconciliation or complete auction/sale register was found for all 4,704 vehicles. The remaining vehicles and actual realised sale prices are not verified.

### 4.2 Ola Fleet / GCabs

Ola Fleet Technologies Private Limited is identifiable as an ANI Technologies subsidiary. However, the public company material retrieved does not establish a current GCabs-branded operating fleet, the number of active fleet vehicles or current vehicle procurement volumes.

A subsidiary's existence or revenue is not evidence of its vehicle inventory. (`turn642065view3`; `turn828706search2`; `turn955120search1`.)

Use the legal entity name only where relevant. Do not present GCabs as a verified current fleet subsidiary or buyer without a current filing or company confirmation.

### 4.3 Procurement behaviour

This remains a primary-research gap. Public fleet counts do not tell us:

- Who has final authority to approve a vehicle order.
- Whether vehicles are bought, financed, leased or supplied by an OEM/lessor.
- Typical batch size and annual procurement volume.
- Replacement age and residual-value assumptions.
- Whether the operator bears charging capex and electricity costs.
- Whether a fleet is owned by the platform, a fleet partner, a driver or a leasing company.

The most valuable operator-interview question is:

> Who owns each vehicle class, who decides the next purchase, and what operating constraint would make you replace your current vehicle with an EV?

## 5. Charging, airport access and infrastructure — items 29–32

### 5.1 National charging data: use the right denominator

The Ministry of Heavy Industries' 27 March 2026 parliamentary response is the most useful current official source retrieved. It states that Oil Marketing Companies reported **27,737 EV public charging stations installed over the previous five years**, of which **22,753 were operational** and **21 decommissioned** as of 1 March 2026.

The ministry also explicitly says that data for Tier-2/Tier-3 cities and national highways is **not centrally maintained**. (`turn724461view1`.)

Selected operational counts from that OMC dataset:

| State | OMC EVPCS operational, 1 March 2026 |
|---|---:|
| Delhi | 542 |
| Gujarat | 1,118 |
| Karnataka | 2,039 |
| Maharashtra | 1,786 |
| Tamil Nadu | 1,677 |
| Telangana | 1,173 |
| Uttar Pradesh | 2,893 |
| West Bengal | 1,039 |

Source: Ministry of Heavy Industries, Rajya Sabha answer, 27 March 2026 (`turn724461view1`).

A separate December 2025 government reply reported **29,151 chargers installed**, including 8,805 fast and 20,346 slow chargers. That is a different data universe and reporting exercise. It must not be added to or directly compared with the OMC-only operational count. (`turn724461view2`.)

Important distinctions:

- Charger/station versus connector.
- Installed versus operational.
- Operational versus available when a vehicle arrives.
- AC/slow versus DC/fast.
- OMC network versus all CPOs.
- State-level count versus route-level coverage.

These counts do not answer whether a 32 kWh fleet cab can reliably charge on a particular route at the time it needs to.

### 5.2 Airport charging feasibility

| Airport | What public information establishes | Still unverified |
|---|---|---|
| Delhi IGI | Evera is listed at T3, Level 0 of the multi-level car park. | Evera-only depot access, dedicated EV bays, contracted charging tariff, transformer capacity, charger count and uptime |
| Hyderabad RGIA | Dedicated airport taxi fleet is publicly announced. | Whether the 200+ vehicles are EVs; depot/charging arrangement; access rights for an outside EV fleet |
| NMIA | Ground-transport capacity and Bharat Taxi dedicated-fleet plan are announced. | Actual active Bharat Taxi fleet, installed operational EV chargers, charging access rights, tariff and bay allocation |

The NMIA announcement distinguishes overall transport capacity from Bharat Taxi's planned fleet. Do not use the airport's 3,787 app-based taxi capacity as the Bharat Taxi vehicle count. (`turn410223search0`; `turn410223search1`; `turn293234view1`.)

**The most important missing document is the airport concession or fleet-access agreement.** A public charger map cannot establish whether a fleet can park, queue, charge or pick up passengers from the required location.

### 5.3 Airport parking and access charges

Delhi Airport's published parking schedule, effective 15 April 2025, has time-based commercial-car parking rates. Separately, a February 2026 report describes a ₹290 app-cab pickup charge at IGI, attributed to the airport's contractual parking arrangement. These are different charges. The ₹290 reported pickup charge should not be treated as an annual fleet-access fee or a national airport tariff. (`turn885994view5`; `turn821059view8`.)

For each airport, verify: entry fee, pickup fee, parking grace period, queue/holding fee, concession rights, charging access and the entity legally responsible for paying each charge.

### 5.4 Highway and urban charging density

Items 31 and 32 cannot be resolved from the national totals above. The Ministry explicitly disclaims central highway and Tier-2/Tier-3 coverage. State-level station counts cannot be translated into route reliability. (`turn724461view1`.)

For the eight target cities and major intercity corridors, collect a date-stamped dataset of charger location, connector type, rated power, actual uptime, successful-session rate, tariff, queueing and access restrictions. A provider's app/API plus site verification is more suitable than state-wise installation totals.

## 6. Policy and regulation — items 33–36

### 6.1 Central incentives: FAME II and PM E-DRIVE

**Do not model a current central passenger-sedan subsidy without a specific eligibility provision.**

The PM E-DRIVE official portal reports:

- Total sanctioned outlay: **₹11,900 crore**, following an August 2026 enhancement.
- Scheme extended through **31 March 2028**.
- The L5 e-three-wheeler component closed on **26 December 2025**.
- Listed demand-incentive components include e-two-wheelers, e-three-wheelers, e-ambulances and e-trucks, alongside bus deployment and charging infrastructure.

The current published scheme description does not list ordinary M1 electric passenger cars/taxis as a demand-incentive category. A fleet-purchase model for a passenger sedan should therefore not include a PM E-DRIVE purchase subsidy unless a specific eligible subcategory and approval are documented. (`turn953939view0`; `turn724461view3`.)

Source: **Ministry of Heavy Industries — PM E-DRIVE official scheme summary** (`turn953939view0`).

FAME II is no longer a live buyer incentive for a new vehicle purchase. The March 2026 ministry reply reports ₹912.50 crore sanctioned, ₹895.48 crore released and ₹655.43 crore utilised for charging infrastructure under FAME II. These are charging-infrastructure fund figures, not a fresh subsidy entitlement for a new passenger taxi. (`turn724461view1`.)

### 6.2 GST on vehicle purchases

Current rates must be described by vehicle category, not as a blanket “EV 5%, ICE 18%” comparison.

| Vehicle | GST rate | Relevant scope |
|---|---:|---|
| Battery-electric vehicle | 5% | Electric vehicle goods classification |
| Small petrol/LPG/CNG car | 18% | Engine up to 1,200 cc **and** length up to 4,000 mm |
| Small diesel car | 18% | Engine up to 1,500 cc **and** length up to 4,000 mm |
| Mid-size/large cars | 40% | Exceeding relevant size/capacity thresholds |

These revised rates took effect on **22 September 2025**. The GST Council FAQ gives the small-car definitions. (`turn848648view3`; `turn848648view4`; `turn444615search94`; `turn444615search5`.)

For the Dzire Tour S, manufacturer specifications of 1,197 cc and approximately 3,995 mm put the petrol/CNG model within the small-car definition. The relevant comparison is therefore **5% EV versus 18% petrol/CNG vehicle GST**, assuming the vehicle is supplied under the applicable passenger-car classification.

Do not confuse vehicle-purchase GST with GST on the ride service. The latter has separate treatment, including a distinction between a 5% rate without input tax credit and an 18% rate with credit under the relevant service classification. (`turn197453view3`.)

## 10. Implications for MACH1 positioning

The strongest conclusion is **not** “the airport EV fleet market is already proven.” It is more specific:

> There are identifiable fleet operators and airport mobility deployments, but the evidence currently proves fleet presence and operating activity much more strongly than it proves incremental EV procurement demand.

### 10.1 What can remain in the positioning argument

**1. Fleet operators are identifiable potential buyers.** Everest, Lithium Urban, Refex and Evera provide concrete operator targets. Their disclosed fleet sizes help identify relevant organisations, but the next step is to establish who owns the assets and who controls replacement decisions.

**2. Airport operations deserve deeper investigation.** Evera at Delhi T3 and the NMIA Bharat Taxi announcement establish actual airport-related deployment contexts. The economics need site-specific validation.

**3. Commercial EV adoption is supported by policy in selected states.** Maharashtra's M1 transport incentives, Tamil Nadu's transport-EV tax exemption and other state tax measures can affect acquisition economics. Incentives must be applied according to vehicle category, registration location and current quota/eligibility rules.

**4. TCO remains a meaningful part of the argument.** The existing Grade A TCO engine and driver advantage should remain separate from these unverified external market claims. External research should strengthen the opportunity and procurement argument, not silently alter already-verified calculations.

### 10.2 What should be removed or qualified

Do not present the following as established facts:

- GMR operates 200+ EVs at Hyderabad Airport.
- Bharat Taxi has already deployed 40 vehicles at NMIA.
- BluSmart's whole fleet realised a 26% resale value.
- Airport app-cabs represent 20–30% of Indian airport ground transport.
- The segment-level trip distances, kilometres/day, trips/day, price sensitivities and growth rates in the deck.
- A universal ₹29/day Uber/Rapido platform fee.
- Uber Black's two leading Indian use cases.
- The exact 196.24M AAI passenger figure without the relevant official monthly source and definitions.

## 11. Highest-value remaining research — recommended execution order

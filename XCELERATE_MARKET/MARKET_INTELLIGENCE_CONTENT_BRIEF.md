## B. Market segmentation and operating environments

### Category: Intra-city on-demand mobility

- **Demand structure:** App-hailed, immediate dispatch, point-to-point within city
- **Service tier:** Shared or individual; price-sensitive
- **Booking:** No pre-booking; dispatched on demand
- **Operating organisation:** Platform-mediated (Uber/Ola/Rapido); aggregator-partner model
- **Fleet-management characteristics:** Commission-based revenue to platform; driver utilised across trip purposes; no route scheduling
- **Existing organised activity:** 3-platform daily total 1.62M rides; market-wide ~810M/yr

### Category: Airport transfers

- **Demand structure:** Pre-booked or queue-based trips to/from airports
- **Booking concentration:** ~40% of Uber Reserve trips end at airports; Reserve grew ~200% YoY
- **Service tier:** Mixed; premium positioning relative to intra-city
- **Operating organisation:** Dedicated airport operators (Evera at Delhi T3); aggregator contracts (Uber/Ola/Rapido)
- **Fleet-management characteristics:** Pre-booked demand; pick-up fees at airport; limited deadhead (unverified); queue at airport ranks
- **Key operators:** Evera (Delhi T3, ~2,200 EVs with 55% airport bookings); OHM E-Cabs (RGIA, 100 EVs deployed 2022)
- **Airport cost structure (Delhi verified):** ₹290/trip mandatory pick-up fee (increased ~40%); toll + fee ≈ 50% of short-distance fare (ET/TOI, 2025)
- **Charging infrastructure:** Delhi airport: 12 public stations, 22 points (DIAL blog, Oct 2022); no documented general third-party depot charging at any airport

**Excluded from slide:** All segment table metrics (km/day, trips/day, fares, growth, EV share, price sensitivity) — S-D/C-D, unsourced. 20–30% airport app-cab mode share (assumption, not measured). Empty running / total vehicle-km (unmeasured for all archetypes).

### Category: Contracted corporate commute

- **Demand structure:** Employer-contracted employee transport, recurring routes, scheduled shifts
- **Booking structure:** Pre-scheduled; recurring on fixed routes
- **Service tier:** B2B; predictable demand
- **Operating organisation:** B2B mobility providers (Lithium Urban); shifted-from-airport operators (Refex eVeelz)
- **Fleet-management characteristics:** Depot-based charging; recurring routes; 10–12h shifts; vehicle utilisation ~300 km/day (Lithium Urban, Mint interview)
- **Key operators:** Lithium Urban (Bengaluru, 19 cities, 3,000+ EVs, 600+ captive chargers, 25,000 trips/day); Refex eVeelz (Chennai, 5 cities, 1,750+ vehicles, shifted from airport to B2B)
- **Verified utilisation anchor:** ~300 km/day, 10–12h shifts (Lithium Urban via Mint interview, S-C/C-C)

**Excluded from slide:** Published trip data by any operator (none exist). Contract terms (unpublished). Corporate commute revenue per paid km (S-D/C-D, unsourced).

### Category: Inter-city/outstation

- **Long-distance trips between cities;** premium; low frequency
- Not a shortlisted segment for the slide but included for segmentation completeness

### Premium and shared mobility as service-tier overlays

- **Premium:** App-booked premium service (Uber Black equivalent); higher fare; lower price sensitivity
- **Shared:** Pooled/shared ride (any purpose); lower per-passenger revenue; higher vehicle utilisation
- These are service-tier overlays, not mutually exclusive trip-purpose segments

## F. Strategic market-intelligence conclusion

### 1. Two relevant operating segments

| Segment | Key characteristic | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Airport transfers** | Pre-booked/queue-based demand at airports; verified operator presence (Evera at T3, OHM at RGIA) | Partially verified: operator presence verified; demand pattern qualitative; cost structure partially verified (pick-up fee) |
| **Contracted corporate commute** | Employer-contracted employee transport, recurring routes, scheduled shifts; depot charging | Partially verified: operator scale (Lithium 3,000+ EVs) and depot charging verified; trip data and contract terms unpublished |

### 2. Existing operators establishing these markets are active

**Airport transfers:** Evera (Delhi T3, ~2,200 EVs, 55% airport bookings); OHM E-Cabs (RGIA, 100 EVs deployed 2022); Snap-E (Kolkata, 600 EVs, airport/railway); Bharat Taxi (NMIA, "up to 40" planned, not deployed)

**Contracted corporate commute:** Lithium Urban (Bengaluru, 19 cities, 3,000+ EVs, 600+ chargers, 25,000 trips/day); Refex eVeelz (Chennai, 5 cities, 1,750+ vehicles, shifted from airport to B2B)

### 3. Potential vehicle customers

| Plausible customer | Evidence basis |
|---|---|
| **Lithium Urban** | 100% owned fleet; B2B corporate; expanding; captive charging infrastructure |
| **Everest Fleet** | 23,500+ mixed fleet; Uber/Ola partnerships; bank debt + co-investment model |
| **Evera** | Airport-focused; asset-light (leased); 55% airport bookings; scale unverified |
| **Refex eVeelz** | Shifted from airport to B2B; 1,750+ vehicles; leasing model |
| **OHM E-Cabs** | RGIA airport EV deployment (100 documented); ordered 1,300 EVs (delivery unverified) |

**Most plausible purchasing customer for FleetLine E1:** Lithium Urban (established B2B corporate model, captive charging, proven scale) and Everest Fleet (large existing fleet, Uber/Ola partnership model). Evera and OHM are candidates but with smaller and less verified scale.

**Batch sizes:** Documented deals range from 500 to 25,000 units. No evidence establishes which batch size would apply to FleetLine E1.

### 5. What is established and what remains unverified

**Established:**
- Airport transfers have verified operator presence at Delhi IGI T3 (Evera) and Hyderabad RGIA (OHM)
- Corporate commute has verified operator scale (Lithium Urban 3,000+ EVs, 600+ chargers) and recurring demand
- Pick-up fee of ₹290/trip at Delhi IGI is verified (ET/TOI 2025)
- Depot charging exists at Lithium Urban (600+ captive chargers)
- Tata–Uber 25,000 Xpres-T agreement was executed (Feb 2023)
- EV share of new 4W registrations: 7.3–8.7% (Oct 2026)

**Unverified:**
- Airport cost advantage (pick-up fee incidence on passenger vs. fleet; aggregate daily impact)
- Depot charging access for third-party fleets at any airport
- Actual batch sizes and financing terms for new vehicle procurement
- Replacement cycles and annual procurement volumes
- Whether any operator would trial 50–100 FleetLine E1 units
- Airport-specific charging access rights, tariffs, and bay allocation
- Corporate commute contract terms and payment structures
- Measured duty cycle for FleetLine E1 (200 km/day, 312 days/yr is a design assumption, S-D/C-D)
- App-cab mode share at Indian airports (20–30% is an assumption, not measured)

### Conclusion positioning

Airport transfers and contracted corporate commute are **candidate market-entry environments**, not proven profitable or commercially validated beachheads.

- **Corporate commute** currently has stronger documented evidence: fleet scale (Lithium 3,000+ EVs), captive charging (600+ chargers), recurring routes, verified utilisation (~300 km/day), and established B2B model.
- **Airport transfers** have documented operating presence (Evera at T3, OHM at RGIA) but more unresolved questions: cost structure (pick-up fee incidence unverified), depot charging access for third parties, empty running/total vehicle-km, and airport cost advantage thesis (lowest revenue per paid trip-km of 5 archetypes at ₹17.1).

The two channels could diversify demand risk for a single vehicle platform, but this complementarity remains a hypothesis requiring validation through fleet procurement interviews, airport cost quantification, and depot charging feasibility assessment.

**Validation conditions (P0 gaps):** Fleet procurement interviews (5–8 operators); airport cost quantification (pick-up fee incidence); depot charging feasibility for third-party fleets.

# MARKET STRATEGY — SINGLE SLIDE CONTENT

## Headline
**Airport transfers and contracted corporate commute are two promising entry opportunities for FleetLine E1 — different routes to managed, repeatable, EV-compatible fleet operations.**

## Supporting thesis
India's ride-service market is large (591.3M annualised platform cab rides, 3 platforms) but the segment data is unsourced. Airport and corporate commute offer structurally different demand profiles — pre-booked/recurring vs. contracted/scheduled — that may suit a single vehicle platform. This is a candidate direction, not a proven conclusion.

## Section 01 — Market signal

**Airport transfers**
- Airport traffic: ~196M passenger movements Apr–Sep 2026 (S-C; exact 196.24M unverified)
- Airport is not a niche: if 20–30% of passengers use app-cabs (assumption, not measured), airport trips could be 78.5M–117.8M passenger journeys (not vehicle trips)
- Verified operator activity: Evera at Delhi T3 (55% of bookings are airport pick-ups, avg ₹600/trip); Bharat Taxi at NMIA (600 cabs phase 1); OHM E-Cabs at Hyderabad (100 EVs deployed 2022)
- Airport has the lowest gross revenue per paid trip-km (₹17.1) of five archetypes — margin thesis must rest on cost (zero deadhead, depot charging, no surge chasing), which is unquantified
- Delhi airport pick-up fee: ₹290/trip (verified; incidence unverified)

**Corporate commute**
- Lithium Urban: 3,000+ EVs, 25,000 trips/day, 100% owned fleet, B2B corporate transport, ~300 km/day utilisation
- Refex eVeelz: 1,750+ vehicles, shifted from airport to B2B (airport exit is a warning signal for airport thesis)
- Everest Fleet: 23,500+ mixed fleet, Uber's largest India partner, co-invested by Uber ($20M+$30M)
- Contracted transport offers recurring routes, fixed schedules, and depot-based charging — but these are inferred from operator models, not measured

**Intra-city benchmark (for context)**
- Highest volume segment: 591.3M annualised platform cab rides (3 platforms)
- But segment-level data (km/day, trips/day, fares) is entirely unsourced (S-D/C-D)

## Section 02 — Target customer

**Buyer**: Fleet operators and transport contractors (not individual drivers)
- Everest Fleet: 23,500+ mixed (2,000+ EV, 18,000+ CNG) — largest potential buyer
- Lithium Urban: 3,000+ EVs, 600+ chargers — corporate commute specialist
- Refex eVeelz: 1,750+ vehicles — shifted to B2B
- Evera: ~2,200 EVs + 500 repossessed — airport-focused
- OHM E-Cabs: 100 EVs deployed at RGIA — only independently documented airport EV deployment

**Decision-maker**: Fleet-operator founders/CEOs (Trivedi at Evera, Ladsariya at Everest) — buying authority inferred from press statements, not procurement documentation

**Ownership models**: Mixed — owned (Lithium), leased (Evera), financed (OHM via Mufin), cooperative (Bharat Taxi), driver-partner (Ola Fleet). No single model dominates.

**Procurement evidence**: Documented channels include leasing from lenders, bank-financed ownership, OEM-platform bulk orders (Tata–Uber 25,000; Tata–BluSmart 10,000), Uber co-investment, and repossessed-asset acquisition. Batch sizes 500–25,000. Replacement cycles and annual procurement volumes unverified.

## Section 03 — Entry strategy

**Priority 1 (Airport)**: Delhi IGI + NMIA + Hyderabad RGIA
- Verified operator presence: Evera (T3), Bharat Taxi (NMIA, 600 phase 1), OHM (RGIA, 100 EVs deployed)
- Constraints: pick-up fee ₹290/trip (Delhi), no documented general depot charging for third parties, deadhead/repositioning unmeasured

**Priority 2 (Corporate commute)**: Bengaluru, Delhi, Mumbai (Lithium Urban, Refex, Everest)
- Verified: Lithium 3,000+ EVs, 25,000 trips/day, 600+ chargers; Refex shifted from airport to B2B; Everest 23,500+ mixed fleet
- Constraints: contract duration, payment structure, renewal terms unverified; no published corporate commute trip data

**Validation condition**: Fleet procurement interviews to establish actual batch sizes, financing terms, replacement cycles, and whether any operator would trial 50–100 units.

## Bottom-line conclusion
FleetLine E1 is a credible candidate for airport-transfer and contracted-commute operations. Fleet operators of sufficient scale exist (Everest 23,500+, Lithium 3,000+, Refex 1,750+, Evera ~2,700). Airport deployments are real but small. The airport margin thesis is unproven (lowest revenue per paid trip-km, pick-up fees, no documented depot charging). Corporate commute offers more predictable utilisation but lacks published trip data. The recommendation is a candidate direction requiring validation, not a proven conclusion.

## 1. Research Objective and Method

This master research synthesises all prior research for the XCELerate 2026 E-Track challenge. The objective is to determine whether Airport transfers and Contracted corporate commute are two complementary candidate beachheads for a sub-4m compact sedan BEV (45 kWh, depot-charged) designed for Indian fleet operators by 2035.

**Method:** Treat all existing research as hypotheses to verify, not facts to preserve. Prioritise original government/regulatory/company/industry sources. Cite every statistic. Distinguish reported data, calculations, estimates, forecasts, assumptions. Never invent or silently modify figures. Preserve raw data and traceability.

**Evidence grading (two-dimensional):**
- Source quality: S-A (government/regulatory/primary), S-B (industry analyst/report), S-C (company-reported), S-D (unsourced/analyst-assigned)
- Calculation quality: C-A (directly verifiable), C-B (derived with stated method), C-C (derived with unstated assumptions), C-D (no calculation basis)

## 2. Market Landscape — Five Operating Archetypes

### 2.1 Archetype Definitions

| Archetype | Definition | Service tier |
|---|---|---|
| Intra-city on-demand | App-hailed, immediate dispatch, point-to-point within city | Shared or individual; price-sensitive |
| Contracted corporate commute | Employer-contracted employee transport, recurring routes, scheduled shifts | B2B; predictable demand |
| Airport transfers | Pre-booked or queue-based trips to/from airports | Mixed; premium positioning |
| Inter-city/outstation | Long-distance trips between cities | Premium; low frequency |
| Premium individual mobility | App-booked premium service (Uber Black equivalent) | High fare; low price sensitivity |
| Shared mobility | Pooled/shared ride (any purpose) | Lower per-passenger revenue; higher vehicle utilisation |

### 2.2 Segment Comparison (all metrics from original deck, S-D/C-D unless noted)

| Metric | Intra-city | Commute | Airport | Inter-city | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Avg trip (km) | 12.7 | 22.0 | 35.0 | 280 | 18.0 |
| Trips/day | 7.5 | 8.0 | 6.0 | 1.5 | 8.0 |
| Rs/trip | 350 | 550 | 600 | 4,200 | 520 |
| Rs/day revenue | 2,625 | 4,400 | 3,600 | 6,300 | 4,160 |
| Rev/paid km (Rs) | 27.6 | 25.0 | **17.1** | 18.5 | 28.9 |
| EV share | 10% | 12% | 18% | 5% | 15% |
| Price sens. | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.35 | 0.40 | 0.30 |
| Growth | 18% | 24% | 26% | 20% | 28% |

**Critical finding:** Airport has the **lowest revenue per paid trip-km (Rs 17.1)** of all five archetypes. The margin thesis must rest on cost (zero deadhead, depot charging, no surge chasing) — none of which is quantified. Airport's apparent attractiveness (pre-booked demand, verified operator presence) is offset by the lowest unit revenue.

**Revenue per paid trip-km != profit per vehicle-km.** Total odometer km (including deadhead, repositioning) is unmeasured for all archetypes. The deck's "zero deadhead" claim for airport is unsupported.

## 6. Strategic Comparison — Airport vs. Corporate Commute

| Factor | Airport | Corporate Commute | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross revenue per paid km | Rs 17.1 (lowest of 5) | Rs 25.0 | S-D/C-D (unsourced) |
| Gross revenue per vehicle-day | Rs 3,600 | Rs 4,400 | S-D/C-D (unsourced) |
| Demand pattern | Pre-booked/queue-based | Recurring/scheduled | Verified (qualitative) |
| Empty running | Unmeasured | Unmeasured | Unknown |
| Charging access | Public on premises; captive hubs; third-party access unverified | Depot-based (Lithium 600+ chargers) | Partially verified |
| Additional costs | Rs 290/trip pick-up fee (Delhi); toll; parking | Not documented | Partially verified |
| Evidence quality | S-D/C-D (segment data) | S-D/C-D (segment data) | Both unsourced |
| Verified operator presence | Evera, OHM, Bharat Taxi (small deployments) | Lithium 3,000+, Refex 1,750+, Everest 23,500+ | Verified (company-reported) |
| Scale-up potential | Unverified (no batch-size data) | Unverified (no batch-size data) | Unknown |
| Procurement accessibility | Unverified (no procurement docs) | Unverified (no procurement docs) | Unknown |

**Where Airport has advantage:** Pre-booked demand (Uber Reserve growing ~200% YoY), verified operator presence at airports, lower price sensitivity (0.35), qualitative predictability.

**Where Commute has advantage:** Higher revenue per paid km (Rs 25.0 vs Rs 17.1), recurring/scheduled demand, depot charging (Lithium 600+ chargers), verified operator scale (Lithium 3,000+ EVs), higher utilisation (300 km/day).

**Where evidence is insufficient:** Empty running, total vehicle-km, profit per vehicle-km, contract terms, batch sizes, replacement cycles, actual procurement behaviour, depot charging access for third parties.

**Complementarity thesis:** The two channels diversify demand risk. Airport offers pre-booked demand with verified operator presence but lowest unit revenue and unverified cost advantage. Corporate commute offers recurring routes, depot charging, and verified operator scale but lacks published trip data and contract terms. Both use the same vehicle platform (sub-4m compact sedan, 45 kWh BEV, depot charging). This is a candidate direction requiring validation, not a proven conclusion.

## 10. Bottom-Line Conclusion

FleetLine E1 is a credible candidate for airport-transfer and contracted-commute operations. Fleet operators of sufficient scale exist (Everest 23,500+, Lithium 3,000+, Refex 1,750+, Evera ~2,700). Airport deployments are real but small (OHM 100 EVs at RGIA is the only independently documented airport EV deployment). The airport margin thesis is unproven (lowest revenue per paid trip-km, pick-up fees, no documented depot charging for third parties). Corporate commute offers more predictable utilisation and depot charging but lacks published trip data and contract terms. This is a candidate direction requiring validation through fleet procurement interviews, airport cost quantification, and depot charging feasibility assessment.

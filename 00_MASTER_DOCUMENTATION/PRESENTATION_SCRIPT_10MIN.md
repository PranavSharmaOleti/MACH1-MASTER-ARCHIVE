## [0:30–1:30] Thesis (60s)

> "The problem: design the ideal ride-service vehicle for India 2035. My thesis: volume is in intra-city, margin is in airport and commute. They are structurally different trips, and the vehicle that wins both is a 45 kWh BEV sedan sized for depot charging.
>
> Three findings in one sentence each:
> 1. BEV on LFP costs ₹6.31 per km against CNG at ₹6.75 per km in Delhi — a 14.5% advantage, nominal, excluding infrastructure.
> 2. The driver takes home ₹47,060 per month on EV against ₹32,860 on CNG — ₹14,200 more, every month, at every fare and duty level.
> 3. The advantage is structural, not subsidy-dependent: no purchase incentive for e-4W exists in PM E-DRIVE, and Delhi's road-tax exemption expires in 2030."

## [1:30–4:30] TCO proof (180s)

> "Let me walk the cost stack. 200 km per day, 26 days, 312 days a year — 62,400 km annually. On-road cost: EV ₹12.73 lakh against CNG ₹8.51 lakh — a ₹4.21 lakh disadvantage. But energy: EV ₹2.00 lakh against CNG ₹9.48 lakh — a ₹7.48 lakh advantage. Maintenance: ₹0.94 lakh against ₹2.10 lakh. Finance: ₹16.22 lakh against ₹10.85 lakh. Residual: −₹3.31 lakh against −₹3.41 lakh. Net: EV ₹17.95 lakh against CNG ₹21.01 lakh — a ₹3.05 lakh advantage over 5 years, or ₹6.31 per km against ₹6.75 per km.
>
> Add the depot charger at ₹99,000 per vehicle: +6.5% nominal. Discount to NPV at 12%: +6.7% at 5 years, +14.6% at 8 years.
>
> Three cities: Delhi financed +14.5%, cash +22.6%. Mumbai financed +7.6%, cash +15.3%. Bengaluru financed +8.2%, cash +16.4%. Only one negative cell out of 48: Bengaluru, 5-year, cash, −0.4%.
>
> Horizons: 5 years +14.5%, 6 years +20.1%, 7 years +24.4%, 8 years +28.0%. The longer you hold, the better EV gets.
>
> Sensitivity: CNG at 22 km per kg (city, AC on) widens to +19.5%. CNG at 30 km per kg (highway, not metro duty) narrows to +7.6%. Electricity at ₹8 per kWh: +7.1%. At ₹10: +2.9%. At ₹12 — public DC: −1.4%. At ₹18: −14.1%. The break-even is ₹11 per kWh — above that, EV loses. Depot tariffs are ₹4.50 to ₹9.50 per kWh. That is why depot charging is not optional.
>
> Worst case stacked — 15.38 kWh per 100 km consumption, ₹12 per kWh, 26% residual: −6.4%. Best case — 12.84, ₹4.50, 36% residual: +20.6%."

## [4:30–6:30] Powertrain (120s)

> "Powertrain: 45 kWh LFP, PMSM 80 bhp, 62,400 km per year. 12.84 kWh per 100 km consumption. 10% battery reserve — 40.5 kWh usable. 15% design range reserve. 48 degree Celsius cell design point — 44.1 kWh usable, 71.9% SoH at year 8.
>
> Range: 268 km at 15% reserve, 315 km at zero reserve. Overnight: 25.68 kWh to recharge, 7.4 kW AC, 3.86 hours. One port per vehicle, 1:3 fleet utilization.
>
> Why LFP over sodium-ion: no sodium-ion cell has been certified to IS 16893 Parts 2 and 3. AIS-038 Rev 2 Amendment 3, Annex IX-K(2) covers lithium-ion. Reliance Jamnagar: 40 GWh Phase 1, commissioning H2 2026, full site 120 GWh. Naumann square-root-t gives 13.3 years to 80% at 40 degrees. Lewerenz linear gives 3.7 years. That is a 3.6x disagreement, and it is why gate G3 requires 12 months of per-cell SoH telemetry before we sign an 8-year capacity-retention guarantee.
>
> Against the Dzire Tour S CNG: BEV costs ₹6.42 per km against CNG at ₹7.42 to ₹8.34 per km. HEV and PHEV at 43% GST are not competitive. Bio-CNG has supply risk — 207 plants commissioned against a 5,000 target, only 17 pipeline-connected."

## [6:30–8:00] Driver and market (90s)

> "Driver P&L: gross revenue ₹11 per km times 5,200 km equals ₹57,200. Platform fee ₹67 per day times 26 equals ₹1,742. Energy: EV ₹3,338 against CNG ₹15,808. Maintenance: ₹1,560 against ₹3,500. Insurance: ₹3,500 against ₹3,290. Take-home: ₹47,060 against ₹32,860 — ₹14,200 advantage. During the loan: ₹20,021 against ₹14,768 — ₹5,253 advantage. After the loan, year 6 onward: back to ₹47,060 against ₹32,860.
>
> The advantage holds at every fare and duty level. At ₹11 per km the driver gains 43%. At ₹22 per km, 16%. At 150 km per day, EV take-home is 145% of CNG's. At 300 km per day, 141%.
>
> Market: 58% of rides are intra-city street hail. 19% combined airport and corporate commute. Intra-city EV share is 10%, price sensitivity 0.85. Margin segment EV share 18%, price sensitivity 0.30 to 0.35. Airport transfers are the fastest-growing segment at 19.14% CAGR. The Dzire Tour S is the comparator: 3,614 outlets — actually 3,400 plus in 2026 — 30 to 35% fleet share, 5,000 units per month."

## [8:00–9:00] AI and roadmap (60s)

> "AI layer: predictive maintenance gives 7 days advance warning — worth about ₹133 per vehicle per month. Smart dispatch cuts deadhead 15% — ₹50 to ₹832. Smart charging cuts charging cost 15 to 18% — ₹501 to ₹601. Combined: ₹824 to ₹1,776 per vehicle per month, or 5.8 to 12.5% of the driver advantage. Incremental, not transformative. The star is per-cell SoH telemetry tied to gate G3 — that is our differentiator.
>
> Roadmap: Phase 1, 2026 to 2027, Delhi NCR, 1,000 to 2,000 vehicles, 7.4 kW AC, overnight, no DC needed. Phase 2, 2028 to 2030, three cities, 15,000 to 25,000 cumulative. Phase 3, 2031 to 2035, pan-India, 100,000 plus. Five gates. The critical one is G3: 12 months of per-cell SoH telemetry, 3.6x disagreement in published LFP studies."

---

## [9:00–10:00] Risks and close (60s)

> "Risks we cannot hedge:
> 1. Battery degradation. If Lewerenz is right — 3.7 years to 80% — we do not sign the 8-year guarantee. Gate G3 exists for this.
> 2. Electricity price. Above ₹11 per kWh, EV loses. Depot tariffs must stay at ₹4.50 to ₹9.50.
> 3. Charging queues. 75 hours per month without depot charging kills the advantage. Depot charging is mandatory.
> 4. Policy cliff. Delhi and Maharashtra incentives expire 31 March 2030 — five years before our 2035 target. The TCO advantage survives without them: +6.7% NPV with the charger included.
> 5. Hybrid Dzire. If Maruti launches a strong hybrid Dzire in India, the comparison changes. Our response: total cost per km, not energy cost alone. CNG energy is ₹3.04 per km; a 30 km per litre hybrid at ₹95 per litre is also about ₹3 per km. The difference is maintenance, insurance, and the GST position.
>
> Close: FleetLine E1 is a 45 kWh BEV sedan, sized for depot charging, profitable at 200 km per day, with a ₹14,200 monthly driver advantage, defensible in three cities, and gated on battery telemetry before we commit to an 8-year guarantee. Thank you."

## Claim 1 — National Critical Mineral Mission: ₹16,300 crore (Slide 6)

**Exact claim as in deck:** "NATIONAL CRITICAL MINERAL MISSION — Approved in January 2025, the mission provides for ₹16,300 crore in public expenditure over seven years, with an expected ₹18,000 crore of investment from public-sector undertakings and other stakeholders. Its purpose is to strengthen domestic critical-mineral value chains across exploration, mining, processing and recovery."

**What the official source says (paraphrased):** The Union Cabinet (chaired by the PM) approved the National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM) with government expenditure of ₹16,300 crore and expected investment of ₹18,000 crore by PSUs/others. The mission covers the full value chain — mineral exploration, mining, beneficiation, processing, and recovery from end-of-life products. The mission was announced in the Union Budget 2024-25 (23 July 2024); the Cabinet approval and expenditure of ₹16,300 crore over seven years (FY 2024-25 to 2030-31) date to 29 January 2025. The nodal ministry is the Ministry of Mines.

**Source:** Press Information Bureau (PIB), Cabinet approval release, "Cabinet Approves 'National Critical Mineral Mission' ... with an outlay of Rs.34,300 crore over seven years", 29 Jan 2025, 3:13 PM  
**URL:** https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2097309  
**Corroboration:** PIB release PRID=2115222 (26 Mar 2025) restates ₹16,300 crore over seven years, FY 2024-25 to 2030-31, Ministry of Mines.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — All elements (₹16,300 crore public expenditure, ₹18,000 crore expected PSU/stakeholder investment, January 2025 approval, seven-year period, exploration→recovery value chain) match PIB/Ministry of Mines exactly.

## Claim 2 — CEA storage requirement ~411 GWh by 2031-32 (Slide 6)

**Exact claim as in deck:** "ICEA ENERGY STORAGE REQUIREMENTS — India's Central Electricity Authority has estimated an energy-storage requirement of approximately 411 GWh by 2031–32, driven by the need to integrate renewable electricity reliably. The figure was reported in a December 2025 parliamentary response published by the Ministry of Power."

**What the official source says (paraphrased):** In a written reply in the Lok Sabha on 18 December 2025, the Minister of State for Power (Shri Shripad Yesso Naik) stated that the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) has estimated a requirement of about 336 GWh of energy storage by 2029-30 and about 411 GWh by 2031-32 to facilitate reliable integration of renewable energy. The same figure appears in the CEA-notified National Electricity Plan 2022-32 (73.93 GW / 411.4 GWh by 2031-32: 175.18 GWh pumped hydro + 236.22 GWh battery storage).

**Source:** Press Information Bureau (PIB), Ministry of Power, "Development and Deployment of Energy Storage Capacities to Power Reliable Renewable Future", 18 Dec 2025, 4:36 PM  
**URL:** https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2205959&reg=3&lang=1  
**Primary question paper:** Lok Sabha Unstarred Question No. 3093, answered 18.12.2025 — https://powermin.gov.in/sites/default/files/uploads/LS18122025_Eng.pdf  
**CEA NEP 2022-32 notification:** PIB PRID=1928750 (2023) — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1928750&reg=48&lang=2

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — ~411 GWh by 2031-32 attributed to CEA; the "December 2025 parliamentary response by the Ministry of Power" is real and verbatim-consistent (18 Dec 2025 Lok Sabha written reply).

## Claim 3 — CATL Naxtra chemistry = Prussian White (Slide 6)

**Exact claim as in deck:** "WALL 1 - AVAILABILITY AT SCALE — ... CATL's Naxtra has GWh-scale capacity and passenger-car certification, but uses Prussian White—not the layered-oxide chemistry specified for MACH1. These chemistries are not interchangeable drop-in solutions."

**What the source says (paraphrased):** CATL unveiled its Naxtra sodium-ion battery brand at its Super Tech Day in Shanghai on 21 April 2025, with mass production scheduled from December 2025; the passenger-EV cell is rated at 175 Wh/kg (approaching LFP), >10,000 cycles, -40°C to +70°C operation. Multiple technical sources identify the Naxtra cathode as Prussian white (sodium iron-based, Ni/Co-free) with a hard-carbon anode — i.e., not layered oxide. In September 2025 Naxtra passed China's national standard certification GB 38031-2025, and CATL/Changan announced a mass-production sodium-ion passenger vehicle (Changan Nevo A06, ~45 kWh Naxtra pack) for mid-2026. CATL is adding multi-gigawatt Naxtra capacity (e.g., 40 GWh at Fuding, scaling toward 100 GWh by 2030 per industry estimates).

**Sources:** 
- Yicai Global, 22 Apr 2025 — https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/catl-to-become-industrys-first-to-mass-produce-sodium-ion-batteries-this-year (announcement 21 Apr 2025, mass production December)
- EV Infrastructure News, 6 Feb 2026 (GB 38031-2025 certification Sept 2025; Changan mass-production passenger vehicle mid-2026) — https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/ev-technology/catl-s-sodium-ion-battery-launched-in-world-first-mass-production-passenger-vehicle
- Intercalation Station, 26 Aug 2026 (Naxtra = Prussian-white based, 175 Wh/kg) — https://intercalationstation.substack.com/p/rest-ill-handle-the-grid-tonight
- Wikipedia, Sodium-ion battery (Naxtra launched April 2025, 175 Wh/kg, GB 38031-2025 certification Sept 2025) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium-ion_battery

**Caveat:** CATL is a Chinese company; no Indian government source exists for this claim (none is needed — it is a product-chemistry claim). One secondary source (Born to Engineer, May 2026) describes Naxtra's cathode as "layered oxide composite," conflicting with the preponderance of technical sources naming Prussian white.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — Naxtra is sodium-ion on a Prussian-white cathode (not layered oxide), announced April 2025 with GWh-scale mass production from December 2025, nationally certified (GB 38031-2025, Sept 2025) and slated for passenger cars (Changan Nevo A06, mid-2026).

## Claim 4 — AIS-038 Rev.2 Amendment 3, Annex IX-K(2) scope (Slide 6)

**Exact claim as in deck:** "AIS-038 REV.2 AMENDMENT 3, ANNEX IX-K(2), VERBATIM: 'CELLS USED TO MAKE REESS, SHALL BE CERTIFIED AS PER IS 16893-PART 2 AND PART 3 BY NABL ACCREDITED LAB OR BY TEST AGENCY NOTIFIED UNDER CMV RULE 126.' IS 16893-2 AND -3 ARE TITLED 'SECONDARY LITHIUM-ION CELLS…'. NO AMENDMENT THROUGH AMD 4 HAS ADDED AN ALTERNATIVE-CHEMISTRY CLAUSE OR SCOPE EXEMPTION. NO SODIUM-ION CELL HAS EVER BEEN CERTIFIED TO THEM."

**What the source says (paraphrased):** The MoRTH document "Amendment 3 to AIS-038 (09/2022) — Specific Requirements for M, N Category Electric Power Train Vehicles" substitutes Annexure IX-K ("Technical Requirements for Traction Battery (REESS) of M, N Category Electric Power Train Vehicles"). Clause 2 states verbatim: "Cells used to make REESS, shall be certified as per as per IS 16893-Part 2 and Part 3 by NABL accredited lab or by test agency notified under CMV Rule 126." — an exact match to the deck's quotation. IS 16893 Part 2 covers performance testing of lithium-ion cells for EV propulsion; Part 3 covers safety requirements for lithium-ion cells (aligned with IEC 62660). The framework applies specifically to lithium-ion cells; ARAI's Annual Report 2024-25 lists an "Amendment 4 to AIS-038 (Rev.2)" as having been issued, but its public text was not accessible to confirm or deny any alternative-chemistry clause, and the claim that "no sodium-ion cell has ever been certified" to IS 16893 is an absolute negative not verifiable from public sources.

**Source:** "Amendment 3 to AIS-038 (09/2022)" (MoRTH/AIS document, mirrored by ACMA) — https://www.acma.in/uploads/ciculer-attachement/Amd_3_AIS%20038_Rev_2.pdf  
**IS 16893 scope:** Standphill India compliance summary (IS 16893 Part 2 = performance testing of lithium-ion cells; Part 3 = safety requirements for lithium-ion cells, IEC 62660-aligned) — https://www.standphillindia.in/product/ev-traction-battery-pack.php  
**Amendment 4 existence:** ARAI Annual Report 2024-25 — https://www.araiindia.com/MediaFiles/AR_English_2024_25_150925_12335.pdf  
**MoRTH implementation:** PIB, S.O. 4567(E) dated 28.09.2022, amendments applicable from 01.12.2022 — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2225866&reg=1&lang=1

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (core claim)** — The verbatim Annex IX-K clause 2 text and the IS 16893 Parts 2 & 3 (lithium-ion cells) reference are confirmed exactly from Amendment 3 to AIS-038 (09/2022). The deck's absolute sub-claims (no exemption added through AMD 4; no sodium-ion cell ever certified) cannot be independently verified from public sources and are treated as unverified assertions rather than disproven facts.

## Claim 5 — Maharashtra EV Policy: 15% / ₹2 lakh cap / 25,000 units / toll exemptions (Slides 10–11)

**Exact claim as in deck (POLICY table):** "Maharashtra — 15% of ex-factory, capped ₹ 2 lakh, for transport e-4W — 25,000 units; 100% toll exemption on Mumbai–Pune Expressway and Samruddhi Mahamarg; EV road-tax exempt. 31-Mar-2030"

**What the official source says (paraphrased):** Government Resolution No. MVR-0125/C.R.13/Tra-2 (Home/Transport Department, Maharashtra), dated 23 May 2025, notifies the Maharashtra Electric Vehicle Policy 2025 for 01.04.2025 to 31.03.2030. The demand-incentive table lists "Electric four-wheeler (transport) (M1): 15% [of ex-factory price], 25,000 vehicles, max ₹2,00,000 per vehicle"; incentives are disbursed as a percentage of ex-factory price. All EVs sold and registered in the state during the policy period get 100% motor-vehicle-tax exemption and registration-fee waiver. Section 4.2 grants 100% toll exemption for passenger EVs on the Mumbai–Pune (Yashwantrao Chavan) Expressway, the Samruddhi Mahamarg, and the Atal Setu.

**Source (primary):** Government of Maharashtra, Transport Department, EV Policy 2025 GR dated 23-05-2025, GR No. MVR-0125/C.R.13/Tra-2 — https://transport.maharashtra.gov.in/Site/Upload/GR/EV%20Policy%202025%20dated%2023-05-2025.pdf  
**Publication date:** GR dated 23 May 2025 (Cabinet approval 29 April 2025; policy period 1 Apr 2025 – 31 Mar 2030)  
**Corroboration:** Economic Times, 24 May 2025 (₹2 lakh for transport e-4W; 25,000 units; 100% MV tax exemption; toll exemption) — https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/renewables/maharashtra-rolls-out-new-ev-policy-targets-30-pc-adoption-by-2030/articleshow/121383257.cms  
Grant Thornton Bharat alert, 27 May 2025 (15% ex-factory for transport four-wheelers, up to ₹2 lakh; toll exemption on Mumbai–Pune, Samruddhi Mahamarg, Atal Setu; policy to 31 March 2030) — https://www.grantthornton.in/globalassets/1.-member-firms/india/assets/pdfs/alerts/government_of_maharashtra_notifies_the_maharashtra_electric_vehicle_policy_2025.pdf

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — Every element (15% of ex-factory, ₹2 lakh cap, 25,000 transport e-4W units, 100% toll exemption on Mumbai–Pune Expressway and Samruddhi Mahamarg (+Atal Setu), motor-vehicle-tax exemption, expiry 31-Mar-2030) is confirmed by the official Maharashtra GR. Minor variance: some press reports name the Mumbai–Nashik Expressway instead of Samruddhi Mahamarg; the GR text names Samruddhi Mahamarg and Atal Setu.

## Claim 6 — Maruti Dzire fleet statistics: 3,614 outlets / 40% fleet share / 5,000 units/month (Slide 4)

**Exact claims as in deck:** "40% OF ALL DZIRE UNITS GO TO FLEET BUYERS — 5,000 UNITS/MONTH AT STEADY STATE"; "MARUTI'S 3,614-OUTLET SERVICE NETWORK"; "THE DZIRE TOUR S IS INDIA'S GOLD-STANDARD FLEET SEDAN — 5,000 UNITS/MONTH, 40% OF ALL DZIRE SALES"

**What the sources say (paraphrased):**
1. **5,000 units/month:** Maruti's Head of Sales & Marketing (Partho Banerjee) stated that of ~1.6 lakh Dzires sold in the prior year, ~60,000 were the Tour (fleet) variant — "about 5,000 units a month" (Autocar India, 8 Nov 2024; Drivespark, 8 Nov 2024). CONFIRMED for the Tour S fleet variant (~37.5% of Dzire volume, ≈5,000/month).
2. **40% fleet share:** DIFFERS. Maruti's own stated fleet share of the Dzire portfolio is 30–35% (personal 65–70%), per Banerjee (India Today, 25 Nov 2024; ET Auto, 12 Nov 2024; Moneycontrol, Sep 2026). No public source supports 40%.
3. **3,614 outlets:** CONFIRMED AS HISTORICAL ONLY / DIFFERS AS CURRENT. Maruti Suzuki's FY2018-19 Annual Report (BSE filing) states "3,614 service outlets." However, the same company's FY2019-20 report shows 3,864 service outlets, and September 2026 press (Moneycontrol) describes Maruti's Arena network as "over 3,400 outlets" with "more than 6,000 service touchpoints." The deck presents 3,614 as a current network fact; it is a superseded FY2018-19 figure.

**Sources:** 
- Autocar India, "Third-gen Maruti Dzire will continue to be sold as Tour S", 8 Nov 2024 — https://www.autocarindia.com/car-news/third-gen-maruti-dzire-will-continue-to-be-sold-as-tour-s-433463
- Drivespark, 8 Nov 2024 — https://www.drivespark.com/four-wheelers/2024/old-maruti-suzuki-dzire-sold-alongside-new-dzire-details-011-061107.html
- India Today, 25 Nov 2024 (fleet share 30–35%) — https://www.indiatoday.in/auto/cars/story/with-new-dzire-maruti-suzuki-looks-to-retain-grip-on-compact-sedan-segment-2639752-2024-11-25
- ET Auto, 12 Nov 2024 (fleet ~30–35%; FY23-24 Dzire sales 1.64 lakh, ~57,000 taxi segment) — https://auto.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/passenger-vehicle/maruti-suzuki-bets-big-on-new-dzire-aims-to-revive-sedan-sales-invests-inr-1000-cr/115198033
- Maruti Suzuki Annual Report 2018-19 (BSE): 3,614 service outlets — https://www.bseindia.com/bseplus/annualreport/532500/5325000319.pdf
- Moneycontrol, 16 Sep 2026 (current network: 3,400+ outlets, 6,000+ service touchpoints; fleet 30–35%) — https://www.moneycontrol.com/automobile/the-dzire-paradox-how-a-sedan-rules-india-s-suv-dominated-car-market-article-14031208.html

**Verdict: DIFFERS** — The 5,000 units/month Tour S figure checks out (Maruti's own ~60,000/yr ≈ 5,000/month). The 40% fleet-share claim differs from Maruti's stated 30–35%. The 3,614-outlet figure is a real but superseded FY2018-19 annual-report number (current service network is larger/differently reported), so it does not support the deck's present-tense claim.

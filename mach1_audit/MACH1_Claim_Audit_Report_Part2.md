## Claim 1 — ACC PLI scheme: "1.4 of 50 GWh delivered (2.8%)"

**Claim as stated in deck:** ACC PLI scheme has delivered only 1.4 GWh of the 50 GWh target (2.8% commissioning), per IEEFA/PIB Oct 2025.

**Latest source finding (paraphrased):** The ACC PLI scheme (₹18,100 crore outlay, 50 GWh target) remains severely behind schedule. As of October 2025, only 1.4 GWh (2.8%) was commissioned — entirely by Ola Electric at its Krishnagiri, Tamil Nadu plant — with zero incentive payouts disbursed against the ~₹2,900 crore targeted. A Lok Sabha reply (July 2026) stated only 1 GWh was installed by Ola Cell Technologies as of February 2026, with cumulative investment of ₹5,180 crore and 1,277 jobs as of May 31, 2026. Ola has scaled back to 5 GWh by FY2029. Reliance New Energy's Jamnagar plant is still uncommissioned as of late 2025. The Hindu Business Line (Oct 30, 2026) summarizes "under 2 GWh delivered against 50 GWh target."

**URL:** https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/Assessing%20India's%20ACC%20PLI%20scheme_JMK_IEEFA.pdf  
**Publication date:** IEEFA/JMK report — February 2026 (data as of October 2025); PIB Lok Sabha reply — 21 July 2026; The Hindu Business Line — 30 October 2026  
**Corroboration:** Financial Express, 22 Jan 2026; PIB allotment release PRID=1809037, 24 Mar 2022

**Has the figure changed since original source?** YES — marginally downward. IEEFA/JMK (Oct 2025 data): 1.4 GWh (2.8%). Govt Lok Sabha reply (Feb 2026 data): 1.0 GWh installed. The deck's 2.8% figure remains broadly accurate as of Oct 2025 but is now slightly dated.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — The 1.4 GWh / 2.8% figure is accurate as of October 2025; current status is ~1–1.4 GWh (under 2 GWh), so the deck's claim holds but needs a "as of Oct 2025" qualifier.

## Claim 2 — India public charging: "~35,000 ports / 5.7M EVs"

**Claim as stated in deck:** "India has roughly 35,000 public charging ports for 5.7 million registered EVs — 160 EVs per port" (citing NITI Aayog index, via Cadmus/Uber).

**Latest source finding (paraphrased):** Official figures have moved substantially. A Lok Sabha written reply (21 July 2026, Ministry of Heavy Industries) confirmed 52,718 public EV charging stations as of March 31, 2026 (16,561 with fast chargers), based on the BHEL portal. Times of India (Oct 2026) reports 67,657 public charging points as of August 2026. Registered EVs crossed 1 crore (10,023,396) as of July 26, 2026, per MoRTH's Nitin Gadkari in Parliament (July 30, 2026). EV penetration is 8.26% in FY26. Industry trackers estimate ~6.5 million EVs on road (March 2026), with a charger-to-EV ratio of roughly 1:235.

**URL:** https://www.moneycontrol.com/automobile/india-s-ev-penetration-rises-to-8-26-registered-electric-vehicles-cross-1-crore-article-13989447.html  
**Publication date:** 30 July 2026 (Moneycontrol); Lok Sabha reply — 21 July 2026; MoRTH Parliament statement — 30 July 2026  
**Corroboration:** Yocharge (52,718 stations, Lok Sabha July 2026), Times of India (67,657 points, Aug 2026), MeraEV

**Has the figure changed since original source?** YES — dramatically. Ports: ~35,000 (Cadmus/Uber, Jan 2026) → 52,718–67,657 (July–Aug 2026). EVs: 5.7M → 1 crore+ (10M+). The "160 EVs per port" ratio no longer holds; it is now roughly 1:175–1:235 depending on source.

**Verdict: DIFFERS** — Both the port count (~35,000 → 52,700+) and EV count (5.7M → 10M+) have changed significantly. The deck's ratio (160 EVs/port) is outdated; current ratio is ~190–235 EVs per port.

As of June 2026, 26.59 lakh EVs sold against support for ~28.30 lakh. e-3W (L5) sub-component closed Dec 2025 after hitting target.

**Has the figure changed since original source?** YES — outlay raised from ₹10,900 crore to ₹11,900 crore (Aug 2026 amendment); tenure extended from 2 years to 31 March 2028. The e-4W exclusion remains unchanged.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — Outlay confirmed at ₹11,900 crore; e-4W (private electric cars) exclusion confirmed as still in effect. Deck is accurate but outlay figure should be noted as enhanced from ₹10,900 cr to ₹11,900 cr.

## Claim 5 — Delhi EV Policy 2026: "road-tax exemption, expiry 31-Mar-2030"

**Claim as stated in deck:** "Delhi: 0% road tax for life of vehicle; ... 31-Mar-2030" (slide 10 policy table).

**Latest source finding (paraphrased):** The draft Delhi Electric Vehicle Policy 2026–2030 (released 11 April 2026, open for public feedback) proposes 100% road tax and registration fee exemption for EVs until 31 March 2030 — BUT with a new price cap: only electric cars priced up to ₹30 lakh (ex-showroom) qualify. EVs above ₹30 lakh get NO exemption. Strong hybrids under ₹30 lakh get 50% exemption. The policy also proposes phased bans (no new petrol 2-wheelers in aggregator fleets from Jan 2026; only electric 3-wheelers registered from Jan 2027; only electric 2-wheelers from Apr 2028). The draft is not yet final notified policy.

**URL:** https://www.autocarindia.com/industry/delhi-ev-policy-proposes-no-road-tax-for-evs-up-to-rs-30-lakh-till-march-2030-439442  
**Publication date:** 13 April 2026  
**Corroboration:** The Hindu (11 Apr 2026), India Today (11 Apr 2026), ET Government (11 Apr 2026), NewsOnAir (22 Aug 2026)

**Has the figure changed since original source?** YES — materially. The earlier policy (Aug 2020, extended since) gave blanket road-tax exemption to ALL EVs. The 2026 draft introduces a ₹30 lakh price cap, excluding premium EVs. Expiry date (31 March 2030) is unchanged.

**Verdict: DIFFERS** — The deck states blanket "0% road tax for life of vehicle" with no price cap. The 2026 draft limits the exemption to EVs under ₹30 lakh; premium EVs above ₹30 lakh are excluded. Expiry date (31-Mar-2030) confirmed. Note: draft is not yet final notified policy.

**Latest source finding (paraphrased):** Karnataka Clean Mobility Policy 2025–2030 (effective 11 Feb 2025, 5-year validity): Manufacturers receive capital subsidy of 15–25% on fixed assets (Invest Karnataka states 15% on fixed assets incl. land + 25% for charging/battery swapping equipment up to ₹10 lakh/station). 100% stamp duty reimbursement confirmed (Invest Karnataka, Bolt.Earth). Zero road tax and zero registration charges for EVs (consumer incentive DOES exist — contradicting deck's "No consumer incentive"). 25% capital subsidy for fast-charging stations (capped ₹10 lakh, 500 stations). Target: ₹50,000 crore investment by 2030.

**URL:** https://investkarnataka.co.in/key-industrie/auto-auto-components-and-electric-vehicles/  
**Publication date:** 8 February 2025 (Invest Karnataka)  
**Corroboration:** Bolt.Earth (25 Aug 2026), SpeedCharge (7 Sep 2026), Ecozaar (27 May 2026)

**Has the figure changed since original source?** The subsidy range is now stated as 15–25% (not 20–25%), but 100% stamp duty reimbursement is confirmed. The deck's claim of "No consumer incentive" is INCORRECT — zero road tax and registration charges for EVs are provided.

**Verdict: DIFFERS** — 100% stamp duty reimbursement confirmed; capital subsidy range is 15–25% (deck says 20–25%, close but not exact); BUT the deck's "No consumer incentive" is wrong — Karnataka does offer zero road tax and zero registration charges for EVs.

## Claim 7 — AIS-138 CCS2 mandate: "AIS-138 Part 2 amended 2022 mandates CCS2 for four-wheelers"

**Claim as stated in deck:** "AIS-138 Part 2 amended 2022 to mandate CCS2 for four-wheelers; OCPP 1.6J" (slide 6 roadmap).

**Latest source finding (paraphrased):** AIS-138 (Part 1 = AC, Part 2 = DC) defines conductive EV charging system requirements and is harmonized with IS 17017/BIS. It does NOT mandate a single connector — it adopts multiple permissible DC standards: CCS2, CHAdeMO, GB/T, and Bharat DC-001. The market has converged on CCS2 because vehicle OEMs chose CCS2 inlets, not because AIS-138 forced it. BIS certification is mandatory for all chargers sold in India. OCPP 1.6J is referenced for backend interoperability but the AIS-138 standard itself is technology-agnostic on connector choice.

**URL:** https://dev.to/buildbyravirai/ccs2-vs-chademo-in-india-2026-which-dc-standard-to-pick-and-why-it-mostly-doesnt-matter-anymore-2hmm  
**Publication date:** 27 April 2026  
**Corroboration:** Electray Tech (20 Mar 2026), Bolt.Earth (31 Jul 2026), CEA website, SelectRegs AIS index

**Has the figure changed since original source?** No new amendment to AIS-138 found for 2026. The standard remains as-is: Part 2 covers DC charging with multiple permissible connector standards.

**Verdict: DIFFERS** — AIS-138 Part 2 does NOT mandate CCS2 specifically; it permits CCS2, CHAdeMO, GB/T, and Bharat DC-001. CCS2 is the de facto market standard due to OEM inlet choices, not a regulatory mandate. The deck overstates the standard's prescriptiveness.

## Claim 8 — Green SM Noida: "75 h/month charging downtime (~2.5 h/day)"

**Claim as stated in deck:** "75 hours/month lost to charger queues (GreenSM)" citing Indian Express 1 Sep 2026 (~2.5 h/day).

**Latest source finding (paraphrased):** Indian Express (1 Sep 2026) reports Green SM Limo drivers in Noida waiting hours at chargers — one driver waits ~1 hour at the station daily plus ~2 hours charging; charging takes ~30 min to 20%, ~1 hour to 80%, ~35 min more to full; with long queues charging can take 2.5 hours. Power cuts at the Urbtech Sector 132 facility add delays. Moneycontrol (3 Sep 2026) follows up, confirming drivers sleep in cars to secure charging slots. Green SM says it is expanding its charging network (2–3 months timeline as of the report). No updated aggregate downtime figure beyond the original reporting has been published.

**URL:** https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/noida-cabbies-vietnam-venture-ev-charging-struggles-10858614/  
**Publication date:** 1 September 2026  
**Corroboration:** Moneycontrol (3 Sep 2026), Business Standard (8 Jun 2026), Electrive (10 Jun 2026)

**Has the figure changed since original source?** NO follow-up data found. The original Indian Express reporting (1 Sep 2026) remains the sole source for the ~2.5 h/day → ~75 h/month derivation. No contradicting or updated official data exists.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — The underlying reporting (drivers losing hours to charging queues, ~2.5 h/day with queues) is confirmed by Indian Express and Moneycontrol. The "75 h/month" is the deck's own derivation (~2.5 h/day × 30 days) and remains reasonable based on available reporting, but no independent aggregate figure validates it exactly.
